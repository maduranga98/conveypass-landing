How to Manage Contractor Vehicles at Manufacturing Plants and Warehouses

Contractors10 Nov 20264 min read

Every external vehicle introduces an access decision. A reliable process makes those decisions consistent.

Contractors and suppliers are essential to industrial operations. They deliver raw materials, collect finished products, perform maintenance and transport equipment.

However, every external vehicle introduces an access decision. Site managers need to know who is arriving, which vehicle they are using, whether required documents are valid and whether the visit has been authorised.

A reliable contractor vehicle management process makes these decisions consistent.

Why contractor vehicle management becomes difficult

Many sites build their processes around individual transactions rather than ongoing eligibility.

A vehicle may be registered for one delivery, approved through an email and then return several weeks later. The next guard may have no convenient way to confirm whether the original approval still applies.

Other common challenges include:

  • Incomplete driver or vehicle records.
  • Repeated collection of the same documents.
  • Expired insurance or licences.
  • Different access rules across shifts.
  • Poor visibility into previous incidents.
  • Difficulty identifying vehicles with suspended access.

These issues become more significant when multiple contractors visit regularly.

Step 1: Create a central record for every vehicle

Maintain a consistent record of vehicle registration details, associated drivers, contractor organisations, required documents and current approval status.

Avoid relying on a driver's familiarity with the site as evidence of eligibility.

Step 2: Define your entry requirements

Identify which requirements must be satisfied before a vehicle can enter.

Depending on the operation, these may include valid documentation, an approved gate pass, a vehicle inspection or a delivery authorisation.

Make the requirements clear to contractors before they arrive.

Step 3: Verify vehicles at check-in

Registration is only the beginning. Gate staff should be able to confirm current eligibility during each relevant visit.

A digital workflow can retrieve the vehicle record and apply the site's rules without forcing guards to interpret multiple documents manually.

Step 4: Manage inspections and incidents consistently

Document inspections, record defects and route flagged issues to authorised supervisors. When a vehicle must be suspended, ensure that the relevant access status is updated and visible to gate staff.

Maintain a clear record of the reason for each decision.

Step 5: Review contractor performance

Use available records to identify recurring late arrivals, documentation problems, inspection failures and repeated policy violations.

Discuss recurring issues with contractor managers and establish corrective actions.

What should contractor vehicle management software include?

Look for central vehicle and driver records, document expiry tracking, configurable permissions, inspections, pass renewals, suspension workflows and searchable entry and exit records.
