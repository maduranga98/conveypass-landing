Vehicle Gate Entry Log: What to Record (With a Free Template)

Records24 Nov 20263 min read

A vehicle gate entry log is only useful if it can answer questions later: who came in, in what vehicle, whether they were allowed to, and when they left.

Many sites keep a gate log because they have always kept one. But when an auditor, insurer or manager asks a specific question, the log often cannot answer it. Columns are missing, handwriting is unclear, and exit times are blank.

This guide lists what a useful vehicle gate entry log should record, and includes a free template you can download.

What should a vehicle gate entry log record?

Group the fields into four parts: the visit, the vehicle, the driver and the checks.

The visit

  • Date.
  • Time in and time out.
  • Purpose of visit (delivery, collection, service, other).
  • Destination on site, such as a loading bay or yard area.
  • Delivery note, purchase order or reference number.

The vehicle

  • Registration or plate number.
  • Vehicle type, such as tipper, tanker or flatbed.
  • Owning company or contractor.
  • Gate pass number, if your site issues passes.

The driver

  • Full name.
  • Driver licence number.
  • Company the driver works for.

The checks

  • Licence and insurance verified (yes or no).
  • Inspection status, if required.
  • Unloading confirmed by (name).
  • Guard on duty at entry and at exit.
  • Notes, incidents or refusals.

Record refusals as well as entries. A vehicle turned away for expired insurance is evidence that your checks work.

Common gate log mistakes

  1. Missing exit times. Without them you cannot say who is on site now, or how long a vehicle stayed.
  2. "Checked" with no detail. A tick does not show which document was checked or whether it was in date.
  3. No guard name. If something goes wrong, you cannot ask the right person.
  4. Refusals not recorded. You lose proof that your controls stopped a problem.
  5. One book per gate, per month. Searching across several books for one vehicle takes hours.

See how to keep accurate entry and exit records for audits for more detail.

When to move beyond a paper log

A paper or spreadsheet log records what a guard writes down. It cannot check a licence expiry date or a suspension list by itself. Consider moving to a digital system when:

  • The same contractor vehicles visit daily or weekly.
  • You have had an incident involving an unlicensed driver or uninsured vehicle.
  • Audits take days of searching through registers.
  • You run more than one gate or shift.

A digital vehicle gate pass system fills in most of the log automatically. Vehicle, driver and company details come from the registered record, the checks run on scan, and the guard and timestamps are captured without writing anything.

Frequently asked questions

What is a vehicle gate entry log?

It is a record of every vehicle that enters and leaves a site, including the vehicle, driver, company, purpose of visit, entry and exit times, the checks performed and the guard on duty.

What is the most important field in a gate entry log?

Exit time is the most commonly missed and one of the most important. Without it you cannot tell who is still on site or how long a vehicle stayed.

Should refused vehicles be recorded in the gate log?

Yes. Recording refusals, with the reason, shows that your gate checks are working and helps spot repeat problems with a contractor.
