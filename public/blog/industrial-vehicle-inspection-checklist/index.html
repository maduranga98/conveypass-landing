Industrial Vehicle Inspection Checklists: What Should Security Teams Verify?

Inspections12 Nov 20265 min read

A vehicle entering an industrial site may need to satisfy several requirements before it can proceed to a loading bay, production area or unloading zone.

Without a consistent checklist, different guards may follow different procedures. One shift may check documents carefully, while another may focus mainly on recording the vehicle number.

A standardised inspection workflow helps teams apply the site's requirements more consistently.

Important: This is a general operational checklist. It does not replace legally required vehicle inspections, qualified technical assessments or site-specific safety procedures.

1. Driver documentation

Depending on the site and vehicle category, verify:

  • Driver identity, where required.
  • Appropriate and valid driving licence.
  • Required contractor or visitor authorisation.
  • Any additional qualifications required for the task.

If a required document is missing or expired, follow the site's escalation procedure rather than making an informal exception.

2. Vehicle documentation

Check the documents required by your organisation and applicable regulations. These may include:

  • Vehicle registration information.
  • Valid insurance documentation.
  • Current vehicle access permit.
  • Relevant operating or transport approvals.

Record document status and expiry dates so that future checks do not depend entirely on memory.

3. Vehicle condition

Where required by the site's risk assessment, an inspection may include checks for visible damage, tyre condition, lights, leaks and other obvious safety concerns.

The checklist should reflect the vehicle type and the hazards present at your facility. Technical checks must be carried out by appropriately trained personnel.

4. Load and site requirements

Depending on the operation, verify delivery authorisation, loading or unloading instructions, required personal protective equipment and any relevant hazardous-material documentation.

Different areas may have different restrictions. Make sure drivers understand where they may travel and where they must not enter.

5. Record inspection results

An inspection record should identify the vehicle, inspection time, person conducting the check, outcome and any issue requiring follow-up.

For flagged issues, record supporting evidence where appropriate and permitted by site policy.

A consistent record helps supervisors determine whether an issue was resolved and whether a vehicle should be allowed to return.

6. Define approval and rejection procedures

Create clear outcomes such as approved, pending review and rejected. Assign authority for resolving exceptions and make sure gate staff know whom to contact.

Avoid processes where the same issue is handled differently depending on which guard is working.
