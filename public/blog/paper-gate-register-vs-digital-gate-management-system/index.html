Paper Gate Registers vs Digital Gate Management Systems

Gate passes10 Nov 20264 min read

Recording an arrival is no longer the only requirement at a busy industrial gate.

Paper gate registers are familiar, inexpensive and easy to introduce. For a small facility with occasional visitors, they may be sufficient for recording basic entry information.

But as vehicle movements increase and compliance requirements become more demanding, recording an arrival is no longer the only requirement. Organisations may also need to verify documents, enforce access restrictions and retrieve reliable records quickly.

That is where digital gate management systems offer a different approach.

How do paper registers work?

In a manual process, the guard records details such as the vehicle number, driver name, arrival time and purpose of the visit.

The system depends on the guard entering accurate information and following the correct procedure. Supervisors may need to review paper records separately to identify issues or investigate an incident.

Paper records can be useful, but they do not inherently validate whether a driver's licence or vehicle insurance remains valid.

How does a digital system work?

A digital gate system connects access events with structured vehicle and driver records.

Depending on the product, it can check current eligibility, track document expiry, record inspections and maintain a searchable history of vehicle movements.

This changes the process from simply documenting an event to applying defined access rules.

Actual capabilities depend on how the system is implemented and used.

What about cost?

Paper registers have a low direct cost, but the total cost of manual gate operations can include staff time spent searching records, checking documents, following up on renewals and preparing audit evidence.

Digital systems introduce subscription and implementation costs, but may reduce some recurring administrative work.

The right comparison considers total operating cost rather than the price of a logbook against a software subscription.

When should a business switch?

Consider moving to a digital system if your site:

  • Receives frequent contractor or supplier vehicles.
  • Has recurring problems with expired documents.
  • Operates multiple shifts or entrances.
  • Needs to review vehicle movements after incidents.
  • Spends substantial time preparing compliance records.
