How to Keep Accurate Vehicle Entry and Exit Records for Audits

Records12 Nov 20264 min read

When an incident occurs at a factory or warehouse, one of the first questions may be straightforward: which vehicles were on the premises at that time?

Answering it should not require searching through several notebooks, contacting multiple guards or reconstructing a timeline from memory.

Reliable vehicle entry and exit records help industrial organisations understand vehicle movements, investigate incidents and prepare for internal reviews.

What should a vehicle movement record contain?

The exact requirements depend on your operation, but a useful record may include:

  • Vehicle registration number.
  • Driver or contractor identification, where appropriate.
  • Entry and exit timestamps.
  • Gate pass or authorisation status.
  • Inspection outcome, where required.
  • Relevant exceptions or incidents.
  • The identity of the staff member recording or approving an event.

Collect only the personal information necessary for the stated operational purpose, and apply suitable access and retention controls.

Why are manual records difficult to audit?

Paper records can be incomplete, difficult to read or spread across multiple locations. Searching a specific date range may require reviewing many pages.

Spreadsheets can improve searchability, but they still depend on correct data entry, access controls and a reliable process for recording changes.

Neither paper nor digital records are automatically accurate. The process and its controls determine their usefulness.

Five ways to improve record quality

  1. Standardise the data fields. Use consistent vehicle identifiers, timestamps and status definitions.
  2. Record events promptly. Avoid reconstructing arrivals and departures long after they occur.
  3. Define staff permissions. Ensure each user can access and change only the information required for their role.
  4. Record exceptions. Document unusual access decisions, rejected entries and unresolved issues.
  5. Review the records. Periodically check for missing exits, duplicate entries and other inconsistencies.

How digital gate logs support audits

A digital gate management system can make records easier to retrieve and associate movement events with vehicle and driver information.

Where supported, digital inspection records and photo evidence can also help explain why an entry was approved, rejected or escalated.

Organisations should verify whether a platform offers immutable audit trails, export functionality, change history and configurable retention. These capabilities should never be assumed simply because software is digital.
